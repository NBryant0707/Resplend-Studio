Maison / Salons

Where the maison receives

Four rooms.
One standard.

The atelier in Geneva and three salons, each chosen for its distance from the prevailing luxury quarter. Visitors are received by appointment; pieces are shown once, in daylight, on linen.

  • SalonsGeneva · New York · Tokyo · Dubai
  • By appointmentOne visit · two hours

01

The four salons

Each in its own quarter.

No salons on a luxury strip. No signs at the door. The maison receives in four rooms of the world, each within walking distance of a bench the director knows personally.

The atelier · Geneva

Rue du Rhône 36

Above the maison's own bench, on the second floor, with windows onto the lake end of the street. Stone flags, a stove lit from October to April, and the perpetual sound of the regulator against the far wall. The atelier is not a showroom; it is the working floor where every calibre is assembled and verified. Audiences here are conducted with the door open onto the bench.

1204 Genève Mon–Fri · 10:00–18:00
+41 22 312 00 12 by appointment

New York

East 67th & Madison

A floor of a 1908 townhouse two blocks from the Madison Avenue library. Larger than the Geneva atelier, quieter, and used principally for audiences with owners in the Americas. The director is Marguerite Reignier, who joined the maison from the Paris atelier of a chronograph maker in 2014. She is the only member of staff permitted to remove her watch at the bench. Pieces are shown in the north room, against a wall painted in three coats of bone.

38 East 67th St Tue–Sat · 11:00–19:00
+1 212 555 0188 by appointment

Tokyo

The Okura · Toranomon

A suite on the eleventh floor of the Okura, with a door that opens onto a small garden of black stones. The salon operates, by arrangement with the hotel, on four days each season — sufficient for the register in Japan, which is the second largest in the maison's records. Audiences here are conducted with tea, in the order of a Japanese tea ceremony, and are concluded with the same unhurried sign-off the maison uses everywhere.

2-10-4 Toranomon, Minato Quarterly · by appointment
+81 3 6864 0112 4 days per season

Dubai

The Gate Village · DIFC

A suite on the eighth floor of a building whose architect was instructed, in 2017, to design a salon that did not look like a hotel suite. The result is a single long room with a marble floor, a low daybed, and a wall of books in Arabic, French, and English. The director is Youssef al-Hamad, who trained at the bench in Geneva for six years before opening the Dubai salon. He is the youngest director the maison has ever appointed.

Gate Village 8, Level 8 Sun–Thu · 10:00–20:00
+971 4 555 0112 by appointment
02

Local time

Four clocks in the same room.

Each salon keeps its wall-clock set to its own city. The maison prefers that the visitor be reminded, in the course of an audience, that time is a matter of place.

Geneva · Atelier --:--:--Rue du Rhône 36, second floor, regulator against the north wall.
New York · Madison Avenue --:--:--38 East 67th Street, eleventh o'clock wall-clock, beaten by a Century Avenue two-train.
Tokyo · The Okura --:--:--Suite 11, low-side wall-clock lent to the maison by a private collector in Osaka.
Dubai · DIFC --:--:--Gate Village 8, a single white-faced wall-clock in the long marble room.
03

Visiting the atelier

Two hours, by appointment.

An audience at the Geneva atelier is conducted, where possible, with the maître horloger himself. The visit takes the form of a slow conversation in two rooms — the library, then the bench.

Order of the morning

Library,
then bench.

Coffee is taken in the library; the piece is opened on the bench afterwards.

The first hour is given to the library, on the first floor. The library is a small, panelled room with a single long table, two armchairs, and a wall of bound volumes — drawing folios from 1912 onward, a complete run of the Journal Suisse d'Horlogerie, the maison's archive of correspondence with the bureaux de contrôle. Coffee is taken; the piece is not yet in the room.

The piece is brought down from the safe on a tray of grey linen by the maître horloger or by a senior watchmaker who has been instructed in the visit. It is set on the table in daylight, with the loupe beside it. The second hour is taken up with questions on the movement, the case, the regulation, and the history of the reference. The piece is then opened, on the bench, and the owner is shown the calibre and the watchmaker's signature on the caseback.

The maison prefers that the visit be made in person, where the owner is able to travel. Where travel is impossible, the visit is conducted by post, with the photograph of the movement taken under the bench lamp and the caseback signature scanned at three inches.
