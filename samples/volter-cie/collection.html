Allocation

How a piece is offered.

An edition is published in the order in which it leaves regulation. A piece is not sold through a boutique window; it is offered by name, after a written enquiry has been received and acknowledged by the atelier.

The register receives first refusal, season by season. Holders of a previous reference are invited to apply for the next in the catalogue. A small number of pieces each year are released to new enquirers, in the order their letters arrive. Wait time, in practice, is between nine and fourteen months from the date of enquiry to the date of delivery.

Should an enquirer decline a piece offered in their name, the maison does not return to them for the next. The letter of enquiry is closed. A fresh letter may be written at any time.

04

Selecting a reference

Five questions, often asked.

A short reader for the prospective client. The maison answers in writing; the answers below are offered as a guide, and are not a substitute for a private audience at the salon in Geneva.

The maison does not advise on wear. In the catalogue, grade 5 titanium is the lightest and the most resistant to abrasion; DLC coating adds a harder skin, at the cost of patina. Rose gold and platinum will mark with use, as all noble metals do, and the maison considers this a record of the piece’s life rather than a fault.

No. The dial treatment is bound to the reference. A guilloché silver dial ships with Reference 12.01 in steel; a grand feu ivory dial ships with 12.02 in rose gold; a frosted platinum dial ships with 12.04 in platinum; a black opaline dial ships with 12.03 in blacked steel. Custom dials are not undertaken.

Between nine and fourteen months from the date of a confirmed enquiry. The wait is composed of the atelier queue, the eleven-day assembly by a single watchmaker, the fourteen-day regulation and the booking of the caseback signature. The maison confirms a delivery window in writing at the point of allocation, not before.

Never. An edition, once numbered and delivered, is closed. Should a piece be returned to the maison, it is held in the archive and is not reissued. The maison does not produce “service replacements” or unmarked examples of the reference.

Each piece carries two numbers. The first is the edition number — N of 12 — engraved on the caseback in the maison’s hand. The second is the calibre serial, struck on the mainplate at the close of regulation. The two numbers are entered in the maison’s register at Geneva and remain there for the working life of the watch.
