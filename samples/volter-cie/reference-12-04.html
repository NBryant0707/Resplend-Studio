Reference 12.04
«Platine».

The platinum edition of the Calibre V-12. Twelve pieces, 178 grams on the wrist, the heaviest reference in the maison’s catalogue.

01

The piece

950 platinum, drawn from a single ingot.

The case of Reference 12.04 is milled in the maison’s atelier at Carouge from one ingot of 950 platinum. Forty-seven hours of machining. No two cases share the exact grain.

Reference 12.04

A heavier register.

Platinum is the densest of the four metals in the maison’s catalogue. A piece in 12.04 reads 178 grams on the leather strap — roughly twice the weight of the titanium 12.01. The maison considers this not a disadvantage but a property: a heavier watch sits quietly on the wrist, less given to slide, and reads against the sleeve as something carried rather than worn.

The bezel is polished; the flanks are satin by hand, drawn in a single pass under a pearwood polisher. The lugs are milled as part of the case and brushed lengthwise, so the strap sits flush at the junction. The case is 39 millimetres in diameter and 9.1 millimetres in height, sealed against ingress to 30 metres. The dial ships as frosted platinum; two further treatments are recorded below.

02

Configuration

Three dial treatments.

A piece of 12.04 is offered in one of three dial treatments, specified at the point of allocation. The maison does not change a dial after the caseback has been signed.

Guilloché Silver

A barleycorn engine.

The dial is silvered over a brass blank and engraved by a straight-line guilloché engine imported by the maison in 1947. The pattern is barleycorn — forty-eight lines per millimetre. The dial is lacquered twice and fired at 90°C between coats. Indices are applied in 18k white gold and set by hand.

Grand Feu Black

Enamel, fired seven times.

A grand feu enamel dial is built from successive fusions of vitreous enamel on a gold blank. The maison fires seven layers. Each layer risks the work — a single bubble voids the dial. Indices are fired into the enamel, not applied after.

Frosted Platinum

The maison’s standard.

The frosted platinum treatment is the maison’s standard for 12.04. A brass blank is plated with platinum and finished by hand with a fine abrasive, producing a matte surface that reads the colour of the case. It is the dial treatment shipped to the first six pieces of the edition.

04

Provenance & delivery

Two numbers. One box. Twelve years.

Each piece of 12.04 is finished by a single watchmaker, signed by that watchmaker, and recorded in the maison’s register at Geneva.

Numbering

The two stamps.

Each piece carries two marks. The first is the edition number — N of 12 — engraved on the caseback in the maison’s hand. The second is the calibre serial, struck on the mainplate at the close of the eleven-day assembly. The two are entered in the maison’s register against the name of the watchmaker.

The caseback is also signed, beneath the edition number, by the watchmaker who assembled the watch. The maison considers this signature a record of work done; it is not a brand mark.

Delivery

A box, a key, a certificate.

A piece of 12.04 is delivered in a box of patinated walnut, lined in undyed calf. The box carries a brass plaque engraved with the edition number and the calibre serial. A single brass key is provided; the maison retains no copy.

With the box, the maison writes a certificate of regulation: the rate observed across six positions over fourteen days; the date of signature; the name of the watchmaker. The certificate is hand-written, in iron-gall ink, on laid paper from the papeterie of Saint-Amand.

Service interval

Twelve years, by appointment.

The maison undertakes the full service of a V-12 once every twelve years, by appointment at the atelier in Carouge. The service takes six to eight weeks. The calibre is disassembled, cleaned, re-lubricated and re-certified against the maison’s six-position standard of ±2 seconds per day. The case is not opened. The dial is not removed.

A piece returned for service travels insured and in person. Service is offered to the registered owner at any point in the piece’s life, regardless of where it was acquired.

05

Ownership

Five questions, asked in advance.

A short reader for the prospective owner. The maison answers in writing; the answers below are offered as a guide and do not substitute for a private audience.

A full service is undertaken every twelve years, by appointment at the Carouge atelier. The service takes six to eight weeks and is invoiced at the maison’s published rate — currently CHF 4,800, all inclusive. Interim interventions (crown replacement, strap fitting, regulation check) are billed separately and are quoted in advance.

The maison supplies a written valuation at the point of delivery and at every service. The valuation is based on the price of acquisition adjusted by the published index of the maison; it is not a market estimate. Insurers may accept this valuation, or may request their own. The maison does not warrant a third-party valuation.

The maison does not operate a resale desk. A piece offered back to the maison is held in the archive and is not reissued. The maison will, on request, introduce a piece to enquirers on the register, without commission, and will write a letter of provenance. The maison is not a party to the sale.

The case is sealed to 30 metres. The maison’s standard for 12.04 is incidental contact — rain, hand-washing — and not immersion. The crown must be returned to the closed position before any contact with water. The maison tests water resistance at every service.

Twelve to fifteen crowns, daily, in the morning. The crown is knurled and signed with the maison’s V; it pulls out to the time-setting position only — the V-12 has no hacking lever. The crown should be returned to the closed position with the maison’s coin, provided with the box, to avoid cross-thread on the seal.
