The categories

What the maison writes about.

The journal is published once a season — three or four entries a year, no more. The maison publishes only when a calibre has left the regulation bench, and the writing follows the work.

Atelier notes are the journal's first category. They are written by the watchmaker whose bench the work passed through — Marc Aubert most often, or one of the maison's four named artisans. The notes describe decisions that are not in the catalogue: the order in which a calibre is assembled, the reason a bridge is filed twice, the morning the regulation card is signed.

Atelier notes are published when the maison has something to say that the catalogue cannot. They are the most frequent category. Two to three entries a year, on average.

Horology is the journal's category for the questions of the maison's craft that can be spoken about without compromising the bench. The mainspring's note. The escapement's geometry. The calibration of a balance staff. The category is technical and slow. A horology entry is read at the bench, not at the desk.

The maison publishes one horology entry a season, more often than not in the autumn, when the regulator's work is heaviest.

Provenance entries are drawn from the maison's archive. Plate ledgers, regulation cards, the Observatory submissions, the instrument-timer programme of 1944. The maison's archive is read once a year, in February, and one entry is published from the reading. Provenance entries are the longest in the journal. They are written by the archive's keeper, not by the bench.

The maison publishes one provenance entry a year. The 2025 entry was the Observatory trials, revisited.

Craft entries are the journal's smallest category. They concern a single decision — to polish a face, to leave an interior raw, to read a colour from the mill rather than the showroom. The category exists for the maison's accumulated habits, the kind that are not codified but are observed. A craft entry is two or three paragraphs. The maison publishes three or four a year.

The register

A short list,
kept by hand.

The maison maintains a register — a single list, kept by hand, of the people who have asked to be told when an edition is about to leave the bench. The list is not a marketing file. It is not segmented. It is not sold, rented, or shared. It is held in a single drawer in the maison's correspondence office, in the order in which the names were received.

Editions are offered to the register first. When a reference is completed — twelve pieces, numbered, signed — the maison writes to the register by post, in the order of the list. The first twelve names are offered the first twelve pieces. The list is then closed for the reference, and a new list is opened for the next. The register is the only channel by which an edition is offered before it enters the catalogue.

The journal is the maison's way of writing to the register between editions. Once a season, when a calibre has left the regulation bench, the maison sends a letter to the register: the journal's current entry, set out in print as it is here. The register is not charged for the journal. The journal is not sold. It is offered to the names on the list, who are invited to remain on it for as long as they wish to be written to.

To be added to the register, write to the maison at the address in the footer. The maison replies within five working days. The maison does not write to anyone who has not written to it first.
