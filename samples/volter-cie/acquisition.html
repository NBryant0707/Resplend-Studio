Maison / Acquisition

How a piece leaves the maison

By letter.
By audience.

Editions are offered to the register first. The maison does not advertise availability; a piece is acquired through four quiet stages, beginning with a written enquiry and ending in person at the atelier.

01

The process

Four quiet stages.

Acquisition is unhurried. Each stage is conducted in writing or in person; none of them requires a payment processor, a courier, or a counter.

01

The letter

A written enquiry, addressed to the Direction at the Geneva atelier. Letters are read by hand and answered within two working days. A letter is preferable to a form: it carries, in its handwriting or its turn of phrase, something the maison regards as essential — the temper of the writer. There is no obligation in writing, and no expectation in the reply.

02

The audience

A visit, by appointment, to Geneva or to one of the four salons. The audience takes between one and two hours. The piece is shown once, in daylight, on linen. The director of the salon — or, in Geneva, the maître horloger himself — answers questions without hurry. Nothing is offered that is not yet ready to leave regulation.

03

The allocation

A number within the edition of twelve is reserved. The allocation is binding on both sides once a deposit of thirty per cent has been received and acknowledged in writing. The piece is then inscribed with the owner's initials and enters the bench for verification at the salon of intended delivery. Pieces are not numbered in the order in which they are paid for; they are numbered, capriciously and with care, by the maison.

04

The delivery

Collected, where possible, in person at the atelier. The delivery is a single meeting with the watchmaker who assembled the piece, during which the case is opened, the movement explained, and the caseback signed. International delivery, where the owner cannot attend, is by hand. The maison does not courier; it sends a watchmaker. The wait, between allocation and delivery, is presently nine to fourteen months.

02

The register

Offered to the register first.

The maison writes once a season, when a calibre leaves regulation, to those whose names appear on the register. The register is private. There is no fee, no obligation, and no unsubscribe button — only a quiet removal on written request.

A line of enquiry.

04

Terms

The agreements.

Written plainly, in the language the maison has used since 1912. Each term may be discussed in the audience; each may be varied, by mutual letter, where circumstances warrant.

Allocation is confirmed on receipt of a deposit of thirty per cent of the price. The deposit is held against the piece and credited to the final settlement. It is not, at any point, refundable in cash; where the maison is unable to deliver within twenty-four months, the deposit is returned in full with interest at the prevailing Swiss savings rate.

An allocation may be cancelled by the client, in writing, at any point before the piece enters final regulation. The deposit is, in that case, transferred to the next available edition of the same reference, or to a reference of equivalent value. Cancellations after final regulation are at the maison's discretion; the maison prefers, in such cases, to find a buyer than to retain the deposit.

Each piece is sold with a written valuation at the moment of delivery, signed by the maître horloger and the directeur. The valuation is honoured, against the maison, for a period of five years from delivery, and is the figure used in any subsequent resale or inheritance.

The maison recommends a complete service every five to seven years, conducted at the Geneva atelier by the watchmaker named on the caseback, where practicable. Service is at cost; the estimate is provided in advance and is accompanied by a photograph of each component removed.

Where the owner cannot collect in Geneva or in a salon, the maison sends a watchmaker in person. The watchmaker remains with the piece for the first twenty-four hours in the owner's residence, answers questions on the movement, and returns with the previous owner's written instructions for the next service. The cost of the watchmaker's travel is borne by the maison.

Prices are quoted exclusive of VAT, customs duty, and any importation charge applicable in the country of delivery. Where the piece is to be collected in Switzerland and exported by the owner, the maison arranges the relevant Swiss documentation. The owner is responsible for all declarations in the country of residence.
