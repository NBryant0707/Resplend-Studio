The bench-holders

Six of the twelve.

Six of the twelve bench-holders, photographed between regulation runs. Names, benches, and the years of their appointment. The other six — Jacques, Helena, Pierre, and three not pictured — are described in the maison's internal register.

Maître Horloger

Marc Aubert

Bench 01 since 1994. Trained in-house under Henri Vuille and served at Patek Philippe before returning to Geneva. Holds the maison's only signature plate. Approves each sealed movement before it is boxed for delivery. Has signed every Reference 12 since the 12.01.

02

Apprenticeship

Seven years to a first movement.

The apprenticeship lasts seven years. The first three are spent at WOSTEP in Le Sentier; the next four are spent at the bench, under one master. The maison trains its own.

In-house training

Why the maison
raises its own.

No watchmaker has been hired from outside the maison in the last forty-three years. The waiting list for the apprenticeship is held in the registry; new names are added once a year, in March, by post. Eight to ten are taken each year. The rest are kept on file.

The first three years are spent at the Watchmakers of Switzerland Training and Educational Program in Le Sentier, in the Vallée de Joux. The curriculum is shared with nine other houses. The apprentice learns the common vocabulary — horological terms in French, in English, in the German used by the catalogues — and the standard service operations on third-party calibres.

Years four through seven are spent at the bench, under a single master. The apprentice is not rotated. The master is paid for the apprentice's seat from the maison's own accounts, and is responsible for the apprentice's first movement, which is given to the library on completion. Of the thirty-two apprentices trained since 1994, nineteen are still at the bench. Four have taken benches elsewhere. One — the first, in 1995 — is the Maître Horloger.

The maison does not advertise the apprenticeship. The standard route is by introduction. Letters of introduction are read on Thursdays, in the order they arrive, by Marc Aubert or, in his absence, by the Maître-Adjoint. A reply is sent within the month.

The signature

A name on the caseback.

Every piece carries two engraved marks. The first is the watchmaker's name, in the maison's hand. The second is the regulator's, in smaller letters, set beneath.

The name on the caseback is mine, and the name beneath it is the regulator's. Twelve watches pass through this bench every year. I have signed them all.

Marc Aubert · Maître Horloger

04

Joining the maison

How to write.

Openings are rare. Apprenticeships are rarer. Both begin with a letter.

Open positions at the bench are held against the maison's own roster, not the open market. When a bench falls vacant — by retirement, by transfer to the salons, or by leave — the post is offered first to the most senior apprentice. The maison does not advertise bench positions externally.

Specialist posts — enameller, guillocheur, saddler — are filled once in a generation. The last such appointment was Sébastien Rossier at Bench 11 in 2016. The previous one was Catherine Mégevand at Bench 07 in 1998. Between these two events, no specialist post was opened.

Eight to ten apprenticeships are offered each year. The route is by introduction: a letter from a watchmaker already known to the maison, posted to the atelier with a candidate's name, age, and schooling. The maison replies once the next March intake is set.

There is no application form. The candidate is invited to spend one day at the maison, with travel paid from Geneva. The day is spent at the bench with two masters in turn. The decision is given within the week. The maison does not interview for the apprenticeship beyond this one day.

The salons in Geneva, New York, Tokyo, and Dubai are staffed by senior watchmakers from the atelier. A transfer to a salon is offered after the seventh year at the bench and is held for two to five years before the post returns to the maison. Two salon posts are filled every other year; the most recent were in Tokyo and Dubai, autumn 2025.

Salon hosts are not hired externally. The route is the atelier. A watchmaker at the bench for twelve years may apply, in writing, to be considered for a transfer. The Maître Horloger approves the transfer.

A first letter should be short. Handwritten, on a single sheet, in the language of the candidate's schooling. Letters should be addressed to the Maître Horloger and posted to the atelier on Rue du Rhône. Enclosures are not read in advance.

Include: a candidate's full name, date of birth, schooling, and the name of the watchmaker who has introduced them, if any. Do not include a CV, a photograph, or a portfolio. The maison reads for the letter, not for the list. Replies are sent within the month.
