Engagement tiers · 2026

Three tiers, one fee model.

Meridian runs every engagement against one of three tiers, with a fee structure that is tied to the outcomes negotiated at signing. There is no hourly billing, no junior-pyramid leverage, and no surprise change orders. The fee you see here is the fee you pay.

Stage 01 only

Diagnostic

Two weeks on-site producing a written diagnostic memo. The right engagement to start with when you know something needs to change but you don't yet know what or how.

From

Fixed-fee · 2 weeks · Up to 4 specialists on-site

What's included

  • 12-20 structured stakeholder interviews
  • Frontline ride-alongs (3–5 functions)
  • Financial teardown — revenue cohorts, unit economics, working capital
  • 12-page diagnostic memo the leadership team reads
  • Pre-read deck for the executive team meeting
  • One executive offsite to walk the memo

Not included

Engagement FAQ

Questions we hear most often.

How is the fee structured on an Embedded engagement?

The fee is split into three tranches: 30% at signing, 40% across the engagement months in equal monthly draws, and 30% on the achieved outcome at the embed stage. The outcome portion is paid against pre-agreed metrics — typically EBITDA, gross margin, or agreed-to capital/operating milestones. If the metrics are not achieved, the at-risk portion is not invoiced.

Do you offer fixed-fee Embedded engagements?

Yes, and most of our clients prefer the fixed-fee structure when there is alignment on what good looks like. The fixed-fee Embedded engagements run roughly 25% higher than the at-risk variant; we think the at-risk variant usually provides the better return for both sides, but we don't push a model that doesn't fit the situation.

How long does it take from intro call to engagement letter?

Our typical cycle is 4–6 weeks. The first two weeks after an intro call are an internal scoping exercise where we agree with you on the engagement shape, outcomes, and partner assignment. The next two weeks are the engagement letter, which is a single document — not a project plan in disguise. Negotiations on the letter typically take a single pass.

Can you work alongside our existing strategy or consulting firm?

We do, occasionally — typically when the incumbent firm is doing high-level strategy work and we are running the embedded execution. The split of responsibilities is written into both engagement letters so the seams are explicit. We don't work alongside firms we don't respect, which is a short list.

What happens if the engagement is not delivering at month 3?

At every Embedded engagement, month 4 is a structured strategy refresh. We walk the original baseline against actual results, agree on whether to re-plan, and commit to a written course-correction document within two weeks. This is the single most important checkpoint we run — it is also the one most engagements never need.

What if we want to extend the engagement after month 6?

Most Embedded extensions are scoped at the embed stage itself. The 90-day check-in is the venue for an extension conversation, and we have a written template for what an extension letter looks like. Extensions typically run 4–6 months at the same monthly draw rate, often without the at-risk component (the hard work has been done).

Do you take retainers for one or two engagements a year?

We do, but the firm doesn't aggressively market it. Six of our current clients work with us on a multi-engagement basis each year, on a written retainer that pre-commits a partner and bench hours. We cap the number of retainer clients at eight to keep the model honest.

Why don't you publish hourly rates?

Because we don't bill hourly. Every Meridian engagement is fixed-fee or outcome-fee. The economics are partly about our view that hourly billing misaligns incentives in advisory work, and partly about a practical reality: the rate you'd see hides the fact that a junior engagement team has very different economics from a senior-partner-led one. We prefer to be clear about what you pay and what you get.
