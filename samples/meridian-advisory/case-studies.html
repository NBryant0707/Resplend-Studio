01

Anonymized as Northwind Industrial

A 4.2× EBITDA recovery in eighteen months. From a 110-year-old manufacturer.

Industrials · PE-backed Operational Excellence 18 months

Industry / Mandate

Industrials — precision components. Acquired in a roll-up by a mid-market sponsor, then under-managed through two years of margin compression.

Engagement

Operational excellence stage 03. Embedded five-person Meridian team across ops, finance, and commercial for 18 months.

Senior team

Lead partner: Helena Voss (Operations).
Specialist: Marcus Reilly (Manufacturing).

Engagement fee

$2.6M over 18 months, 35% at-risk against pre-agreed outcomes.

Context

The company had been through two owners across seven years. The most recent roll-up had acquired it with a hold period of four to five years, and a clear value-creation thesis: margin recovery, working-capital release, and bolt-on capacity in the existing footprint. By the time Meridian was retained, EBITDA had compressed by 38% from acquisition and the hold-period thesis was in question.

The CEO and COO were capable operators whose intuition had run out of road. They did not need a strategy firm to tell them what the issue was — they needed an embedded team with the operating muscle to actually run the rebuild.

The engagement

Meridian embedded a five-person team across ops, finance, and commercial for an 18-month operational excellence engagement. The first eight weeks were teardown: SKU-level margin analysis, customer cohort review, supplier renegotiation, capacity audit, and a full organizational-design pass.

The next phase was execution against the operating plan: SKU rationalization (23% of SKUs representing 2% of revenue exited), three-year supplier renegotiation, plant-network rebalancing, and a redesigned sales incentive plan that moved reps from volume to mix.

Outcomes

By the end of the engagement, EBITDA had grown 4.2× from the start-of-engagement baseline. Gross margin expanded 32 points. Working capital released $14M in cash. The hold-period thesis was preserved, with an exit three years later at a 3.4× multiple.

4.2× EBITDA recovery
32pts Gross margin gain
$14M Working capital released
3.4× Exit multiple

02

Anonymized as Verdant Foods Group

A $120M Series D, closed in eleven weeks. From a DTC brand at the cash-eats-growth inflection.

Consumer · Better-for-you M&A Advisory · Capital 11 weeks

Industry / Mandate

Consumer / better-for-you DTC. $80M ARR with high growth, approaching the cash-eats-growth inflection.

Engagement

M&A advisory + equity story development. Strategy and execution in parallel, 11 weeks.

Senior team

Lead partner: James Okafor (M&A).
Specialist: Eliza Chen (Capital Markets).

Engagement fee

$1.4M in advisory + 4.8% placement fee on $120M raise.

Context

Verdant had built a strong brand and a profitable DTC engine. The Series C investor was supportive but unwilling to bridge a round that didn't reset the cost-of-capital picture. The board had three options on the table: bootstrap growth, raise a flat or down round, or run a heavily engineered narrative-led process.

The founder-CEO was skeptical of bankers — a view that was earned at the Series C, where the prior process had been a six-month affair that ended in terms the founder had not championed. Meridian was engaged on the strength of our operating credibility, not our placement credentials.

The engagement

Meridian led the strategy refresh in parallel with the narrative rebuild. Three financial scenarios were modeled (cash-positive, hybrid growth, and full institutional), each with an explicit cap structure and an investor-messaging framework tied to it. The selected scenario — hybrid growth — became the basis for the round.

The institutional investor universe was built bottom-up, with 28 first-meet targets narrowed to a nine-firm deep process across the active window. Materials were written by the Meridian team alongside the CFO, with the founder holding the narrative voice throughout.

Outcomes

The round closed in eleven weeks from engagement mandate. $120M raised at a 2.4× valuation step-up to the prior round. The lead investor introduced through the process became a long-term operating partner to the CEO. Verdant's Series E eighteen months later was materially over-subscribed.

03

Anonymized as Helios Energy Partners

A 32% structural cost reduction, sustained three years. For a renewable operator preparing for institutional capital.

Energy · Renewables ESG · Operations 14 months

Industry / Mandate

Energy — utility-scale renewables. Regional operator preparing for institutional capital under CSRD-grade disclosure.

Engagement

Operations + ESG. Operating-model redesign with sustainability instrumentation built in.

Senior team

Lead partner: Daniel Koufos (Energy).
Specialist: Priya Nair (ESG).

Engagement fee

$1.9M over 14 months, 30% at-risk against pre-agreed outcomes.

Context

Helios operated 1.4 GW of renewable capacity across three regional markets. The operating model had grown organically alongside the asset base, and the cost structure carried the cost of that organic growth. The board had agreed to a private placement the following year, and the underwriting memo would scrutinize the operating cost line on a per-MW basis.

Concurrently, the EU CSRD was being scoped into effect, and Helios — though outside the EU — had voluntarily committed to CSRD-grade disclosure to maintain investor positioning. The two workstreams had to be unified: the operating model had to be rebuilt with the controls and instrumentation needed for institutional underwriting.

The engagement

Meridian embedded a four-person team for 14 months. The first six weeks were teardown: per-MW cost analysis across the asset base, organizational-design mapping, and CSRD-ready KPI architecture across operations, finance, and ESG.

The rebuild touched field-services organization, asset-management functions, and the central operating cadence. Field-services was reorganized from regional to network-of-nodes with a redesigned service-contract structure. Asset-management brought in a new operating model with three-scenario quarterly planning. The CSRD instrumentation was built into the operating cadence from day one — it was not a parallel workstream.

Outcomes

32% structural cost reduction sustained for three years. CSRD-grade disclosure produced on time for underwriting. The private placement closed at a $780M capital raise. The CSRD-grade instrumentation later became a marketed capability for the operations business to its peers.

04

Anonymized as Atlas Specialty Health

A 47% reduction in claims-processing cycle time. For a specialty health operator with nine legacy systems.

Healthcare · Specialty Digital Transformation

Industry / Mandate

Healthcare — specialty TPA. 1.2M members, nine legacy claims systems, regulator scrutiny growing.

Engagement

Digital transformation stage 04. Vendor rationalization + AI integration in claims operations.

Senior team

Lead partner: Helena Voss (Operations).
Specialist: Marcus Reilly (Digital).

Engagement fee

$3.1M over 11 months, 30% at-risk.

Context

Atlas was a specialty third-party administrator with a 30-year operating history and nine claims-processing systems acquired through three different M&A transactions. Cycle-time on a typical claim was 14 days — twice the industry benchmark. The state regulator had flagged the operating variance in the prior two audits.

The CIO had been advocating for a platform consolidation for two years, but could not get past a board that was uncertain whether the costs of change were worth the operational risks. Meridian was engaged to lead the operating-model work that the platform decision depended on.

The engagement

The diagnostic stage instrumented each of the nine legacy systems, mapped the actual processing paths, and identified that 41% of cycle time was non-value-added. The strategy stage modelled three scenarios — vendor consolidation, in-house platform, and hybrid — and the operating-model work in parallel: redeployment of processing teams, retraining, and incentive-plan redesign.

The execution stage ran a hybrid model: vendor consolidation for two systems, in-house platform for the high-volume line of business, and AI integration in the adjudication step. The embed stage transitioned the operating team to the new platform with documentation and coaching.

Outcomes

47% reduction in claims-processing cycle time sustained over three years. 37% reduction in vendor-cost line. Operating variance cleared in the next regulator audit. Annual NPS lifted by 14 points.

9→3Platform consolidation
37%Vendor-cost reduction

05

Anonymized as Linnea B2B Services

A category-of-one story that took share from four incumbents. For a regional facilities-services operator.

B2B Services · Regional Strategy · Growth 14 weeks

Industry / Mandate

B2B services — facilities management. $140M regional operator competing against four national incumbents.

Engagement

Strategy stage 02. Category positioning rebuild and 36-month growth strategy.

Senior team

Lead partner: James Okafor (Strategy).

Engagement fee

$340K fixed-fee, 14-week engagement.

Context

Linnea was a tightly-run regional facilities-services business with strong unit economics and a founder-CEO who had built the business over twelve years. The challenge was not the business — it was the marketing narrative. Linnea's story looked identical to the four national incumbents it competed against, and the procurement officers running national accounts were not differentiating the offering.

The board had been pushing toward an inorganic growth strategy to expand the regional footprint, but the founder was concerned that consolidation would obscure the operating quality that had gotten them this far.

The engagement

Meridian led the strategy stage in 14 weeks. The diagnostic mapped the four-incumbent competitive landscape, the procurement decision-making structure at enterprise accounts, and the actual operating data that Linnea had been under-reporting. The strategy was a category-of-one positioning built around the operating data — the kind of metrics that the incumbents could not credibly claim.

The 36-month growth strategy leaned into regional density rather than geographic expansion, sequenced around organic share-take first and one strategic acquisition at year two. The board approved the plan; the founder-CEO became the lead communicator of the narrative.

Outcomes

41% revenue growth across the 36-month engagement window. Three national accounts moved from incumbents to Linnea after the re-positioning. The year-two acquisition closed with operating and cultural integration successful within nine months.

06

Anonymized as Coriolis Technology

A sell-side process at a 4.1× multiple, in a consolidating market. For a founder looking for the right home.

Technology · Vertical SaaS M&A Advisory 16 weeks

Industry / Mandate

Technology — vertical SaaS. $52M ARR, founder-owned, in a consolidating category.

Engagement

Sell-side M&A advisory. Strategic narrative, materials prep, buyer-process management.

Senior team

Lead partner: James Okafor (M&A).
Specialist: Eliza Chen (Capital Markets).

Engagement fee

$1.1M in advisory + 3.2% success fee on a $214M transaction.

Context

The founder of Coriolis had built the company over nine years, taking it from a founder-led scratch build to a category-leading vertical SaaS business. The category was consolidating, and three strategic acquirers had approached the founder in the prior 18 months. The founder's thesis was that if the company was going to be sold, it should be sold to the partner that was the right home for the product and the team — not the highest bidder.

The engagement

Meridian led the sell-side process in 16 weeks. The diagnostic established the founder's thesis on partner-fit and translated it into a structured process. The strategy stage reframed the materials — shifting from a typical SaaS metrics narrative to one anchored on the operational fit with five named strategic acquirers.

The materials were written alongside the founder; every slide was a discussion between us. The first-round process ran to nine parties, narrowed to a second round of three. The buyer-process management included founder-priority briefings, not the typical banker-led pace.

Outcomes

$214M sale closed at a 4.1× ARR multiple — at the top end of the comparable category benchmarks. The founder's preferred acquirer (the second-highest bidder on price) was selected on partner-fit grounds. The founder joined the acquirer's leadership team post-close and is still there three years later. The team retention rate post-close was 96%.
